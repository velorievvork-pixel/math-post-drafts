**Topic:** The golden angle (≈137.5077°, derived from the golden ratio φ) is
the angle sunflowers, pinecones, and many other plants use to place new
seeds/leaves (phyllotaxis). Because it is irrational, seeds packed at this
angle (Vogel's model, r = √n, θ = n·137.5077°) never fall into repeating
straight lines. Replacing it with a rational approximation (5/13 of a turn
≈ 138.46°, a continued-fraction convergent of the golden ratio) collapses
the pattern into 13 straight radial arms with wasted gaps — demonstrating
why irrationality specifically is what makes the golden angle optimal.
