# Bonus post — 2026-09-11 (extra manual run, requested outside the normal schedule)

**Topic:** The golden angle (≈137.5077°, derived from the golden ratio φ) is
the angle sunflowers, pinecones, and many other plants use to place new
seeds/leaves (phyllotaxis). Because it is irrational, seeds packed at this
angle (Vogel's model, r = √n, θ = n·137.5077°) never fall into repeating
straight lines — the packing stays uniform and gap-free no matter how many
seeds are added. The visible spiral counts in real sunflower heads are
consecutive Fibonacci numbers (e.g. 34 and 21, or 55 and 89).

**Contrast used in the image:** replacing the golden angle with a rational
approximation (5/13 of a turn ≈ 138.46°, a continued-fraction convergent of
the golden ratio) makes the pattern close up into 13 straight radial arms
with wasted gaps between them — demonstrating why irrationality specifically
is what makes the golden angle optimal, not just "close to right."
