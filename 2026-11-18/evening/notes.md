# Evening post — 2026-11-18

**Topic:** The Kakeya conjecture — a nearly century-old question in
geometric measure theory, and its recent resolution in three
dimensions. The classical "Kakeya needle problem" (1917) asked: what's
the smallest area in which a unit line segment ("needle") can be
rotated a full 180° and reversed? Abram Besicovitch showed in the
1920s that this area can be made arbitrarily close to zero, using
constructions now called Besicovitch sets / Perron trees. The modern
Kakeya conjecture asks a sharper question: must any set in R^n that
contains a unit segment pointing in every direction still have full
Hausdorff/Minkowski dimension n, even if it has zero measure/volume?
Proved for n=2 by Roy Davies in 1971. The n=3 case stood open for
decades until Hong Wang (NYU) and Joshua Zahl (UBC) posted a ~127-page
proof in February 2025 (arXiv 2502.17655), widely covered (e.g. Quanta
Magazine's "Once in a Century" Proof Settles Math's Kakeya
Conjecture", March 2025) and built on decades of partial progress by
many mathematicians (Bourgain, Wolff, Katz, Tao, Łaba, Orponen,
Shmerkin, and others). Higher dimensions (n≥4) remain open.
