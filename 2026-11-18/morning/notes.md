# Morning post — 2026-11-18

**Topic:** Conway's Game of Life — specifically the "Glider," the
simplest moving pattern in the cellular automaton John Conway devised
in 1970. The rules: any live cell with 2 or 3 live neighbors survives,
any dead cell with exactly 3 live neighbors becomes alive (birth), and
every other cell dies or stays dead. Martin Gardner introduced the
game to the public in his "Mathematical Games" column in the October
1970 issue of Scientific American; the column reportedly drew more
reader mail than any prior piece in the magazine. The 5-cell glider
pattern returns to its original shape (reflected) after 4 generations
but shifted one cell diagonally — so it glides forever. Soon after
publication, Conway offered a $50 prize for a pattern proven to grow
without bound; Bill Gosper's team found the "glider gun" (which emits
a new glider every 30 generations) in November 1970, winning the
prize.
