# Morning post — 2026-09-18

**Topic:** The Pigeonhole Principle. If you place more items than there
are containers (e.g. 5 pigeons into 4 boxes), at least one container must
hold more than one item — not probably, but with logical certainty, since
5 items distributed among 4 boxes cannot all be singletons (5 > 4).
Formalized as "Dirichlet's box principle" by Peter Gustav Lejeune
Dirichlet in 1834, though the idea appears as early as 1622 (Jean
Leurechon). Classic consequence: in any group of 367 people, at least two
must share a birthday — guaranteed, since there are only 366 possible
birthdays (including Feb 29).

**Deliberate callback:** contrasts directly with the Birthday Paradox
post from earlier this week (2026-09-16/evening2), which showed the
birthday match becomes *likely*
(50%+) at just 23 people via probability. This post shows the same kind
of claim becomes *certain* at 367 people via pure counting — same setup,
two completely different kinds of mathematical guarantee (probable vs.
certain), used as an explicit hook in the caption.
