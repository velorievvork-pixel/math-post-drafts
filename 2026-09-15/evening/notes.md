# Evening post — 2026-09-15

**Topic:** The Monty Hall Problem. Three doors hide one car and two goats.
After you pick a door, the host (who knows what's behind each door) always
opens a different door revealing a goat, then offers you the chance to
switch. Switching wins the car with probability 2/3, versus 1/3 if you
stay — despite the strong intuition that it "shouldn't matter." Made
famous when Marilyn vos Savant answered a reader's letter (Craig F.
Whitaker) in her "Ask Marilyn" column in Parade magazine, September 1990;
roughly 1,000 readers with PhDs wrote in insisting she was wrong, and even
Paul Erdős was skeptical until shown a computer simulation.

**Accuracy check:** verified via web search against Wikipedia's "Monty

retrospective and the original Parade.com writeup — confirmed the
September 1990 date, the 2/3 vs 1/3 probabilities, the reader's name, the
~1,000-PhD detail, and the Erdős anecdote.
