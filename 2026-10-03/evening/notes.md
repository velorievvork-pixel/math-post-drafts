# Evening post — 2026-10-03

**Topic:** The "hat" aperiodic monotile (the first true "einstein" tile).
On March 20, 2023, David Smith (a self-described hobbyist tiling
enthusiast from Yorkshire, UK) together with mathematicians Craig
Kaplan, Joseph Myers, and Chaim Goodman-Strauss posted the paper "An
Aperiodic Monotile" to arXiv. It describes a single 13-sided polygon,
nicknamed "the hat" (a polykite made of 8 kite shapes), that tiles the
entire infinite plane — but the pattern it makes NEVER repeats, no
matter how far you shift a copy of it left/right/up/down. Mathematicians
had sought such a single-shape ("mono-") aperiodic tile — an "einstein"
(German for "one stone", a pun on the physicist's name) — for decades;
previous aperiodic tilings (e.g. Penrose tilings) required two or more
distinct tile shapes. The hat tiling uses the shape in two mirror-image
orientations.
