# Morning post — 2026-10-12

**Topic:** Euler's Identity, e^(iπ) + 1 = 0. It's the special case θ = π of
Euler's formula e^(iθ) = cosθ + isinθ (published by Leonhard Euler in his
1748 *Introductio in analysin infinitorum*), which describes the point
e^(iθ) sweeping counterclockwise around the unit circle in the complex
plane as θ increases. At θ = π that point lands exactly on −1, giving
e^(iπ) = −1, i.e. e^(iπ) + 1 = 0 — an equation tying together five of
math's most fundamental constants (0, 1, e, i, π) with three basic
operations (addition, multiplication, exponentiation). It was voted the
"most beautiful theorem in mathematics" in a reader poll run by *The
Mathematical Intelligencer* in 1988, and placed second (after Maxwell's
equations) in a similar *Physics World* poll of physicists in 2004. The
precise origin of who first wrote it in this compact three-term form is
reportedly still debated by historians, though the underlying formula is
unambiguously Euler's.
